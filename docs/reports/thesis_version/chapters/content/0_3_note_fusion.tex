\begin{notebox}[sharp corners, width=\textwidth]{Nuclear Fusion Reactions in Main Sequence Star}

{\small Accumulation of Deuterium (spin 1) opens possibility for fusing with one more proton to form Helion (1/2 spin, 2 protons and 1 neutron). Rising temperature fuses two Helion atoms to form Helium (0 spin ,2 protons and 2 neutrons) and emits two Hydrogen atoms. This series of reactions from Hydrogen to Helium is called p-p chain (pp I branch) reaction. Other rarer reaction paths can also occur, for instance, pp II branch when Helion fuses with Helium to form Beryllium ( 3/2 spin, 4 protons and 3 neutrons). Then either Beryllium decays to Lithium (3/2 spin, 3 protons and 4 neutron), Lithium fuses with proton and breakdown into two Helium atoms. Otherwise, in pp III branch, Beryllium fuses with proton to form Boron (2 spin, 4 proton and 4 neuton) which is unstable and breaks down into two Helium. The most rare case is pp IV branch, when Helion captures a proton and form Helium. By convention, reactants are placed above the reaction arrow and products below. The energy release at each step is quantified in mega–electron volts (MeV).
%Source: F. Reines, Ann. Rev. Nucl. Sci. 10 (1960) 1-26}
